# Cross-cut: `anti-torque-tail-rotor`

**12 corpus entries disclose this subsystem.**

Earliest disclosure: 1956-10-22

Listed in chronological order. Each entry's `prior_art_notes` and
`disclosure_citation` constitute the citeable prior art material.

## Bell UH-1 Iroquois (Huey) (1956-10-22)

- **id**: `bell-uh-1-iroquois`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Bell Helicopter / U.S. Army
- **disclosure**: Bell XH-40 (Model 204) prototype first flight 1956-10-22; entered U.S. Army service as HU-1 (later UH-1) Iroquois in 1959; ~16,000 produced through 1976 with continuing modernized variants in service into the 2020s. The single most-produced military helicopter in history and the defining turbine-powered utility helicopter of the Vietnam War. Documented in Bell Helicopter and U.S. Army technical orders, Smithsonian collections, and exhaustive published literature.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Bell UH-1 Iroquois (1956) is the foundational disclosure of the production turbine-powered utility helicopter and the two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor at production scale. Establishes prior art for: (1) two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor architecture (simpler than articulated, lighter than rigid; the basis for almost all subsequent Bell helicopters including 206, 212, 412, 222, 230, 407, 412EPI), (2) single-turbine + tail-rotor utility-helicopter configuration at production scale, (3) the cable-with-hydraulic-boost flight-control architecture that dominated helicopter design before fly-by-wire. Bell teetering-rotor patents are long expired. With 16,000+ units produced over six decades of service, the UH-1 establishes that two-blade teetering rotors are public-domain prior art for any eVTOL claim invoking a teetering main rotor or simple semi-rigid rotor hub.

## Mil Mi-8 / Mi-17 (Hip) (1961-07-09)

- **id**: `mil-mi-8-hip`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant / Kazan Helicopters / Ulan-Ude Aviation Plant
- **disclosure**: Mil V-8 prototype first flight 1961-07-09 (single-engine variant); twin-engine V-8A first flight 1962-09-17; entered Soviet Air Force service as Mi-8 in 1967; production continues in 2026 as Mi-171A2/A3. Over 17,000 produced across all variants — more than any other helicopter in history. Operated by ~90 countries. Documented in Mil OKB / Russian Helicopters and Soviet/Russian air-force technical manuals; analyzed in extensive Western literature (Jane's, RAND).
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Mil Mi-8 / Mi-17 (1961) is the foundational disclosure of the highest-production utility-helicopter architecture in history and the canonical Soviet/Russian twin-engine articulated-rotor utility helicopter. Establishes prior art for: (1) five-blade fully-articulated main rotor with elastomeric dampers at production scale, (2) side-by-side twin-turbine arrangement flanking the main gearbox above the cabin (an architecture cloned in many subsequent helicopters), (3) rear clamshell-door cargo configuration for medium-lift utility VTOL. With 17,000+ units produced over six decades, the Mi-8 family establishes that articulated five-blade rotor systems and twin-engine-above-cabin layouts are public-domain prior art globally.

## Bell 206 JetRanger / LongRanger (1962-12-08)

- **id**: `bell-206-jetranger`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron / U.S. Army
- **disclosure**: Bell Model 206A JetRanger first flight 1962-12-08 (as YOH-4A Sioux Scout); civil JetRanger redesign first flight 1966-01-10, FAA type-certificated 1966-10-20. OH-58A Kiowa entered U.S. Army service 1969; LongRanger 1974; 206L-4 still in production through Bell's restart. ~7,300 produced across all variants. Documented in Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron type certificate data sheets and U.S. Army TM 55-1520-228 series.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Bell 206 JetRanger (1962) is the foundational disclosure of the production single-turbine light-helicopter configuration that became the workhorse of civil and military light-rotorcraft operations for six decades. Establishes prior art for: (1) production single-turbine + single-tail-rotor light-helicopter architecture under 1,500 kg MTOW, (2) Bell two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor scaled down to light-helicopter category, (3) the canonical Part 27 small-helicopter design baseline. Bell teetering-rotor patents are long expired. With 7,300+ produced over six decades, the 206 family establishes the production-scale civil-certification path that modern Part 27 eVTOL must engage with.

## Hughes/MD 500 (and MD 520N NOTAR) (1963-02-27)

- **id**: `md-500-defender`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Hughes Helicopters / McDonnell Douglas Helicopter Systems / MD Helicopters
- **disclosure**: Hughes 369 (OH-6 Cayuse) first flight 1963-02-27, entered U.S. Army service 1966. Civil/export derivatives Hughes 500 / MD 500 produced under license worldwide. The NOTAR (No Tail Rotor) variant MD 520N first flight 1989-05-01, FAA certified 1991-09-13 — the first and (with the MD 600N) the only NOTAR helicopter to reach civil certification and production. Documented in Hughes / McDonnell Douglas / MD Helicopters technical manuals and U.S. Army TM 55-1520-214 series.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Hughes/MD 500 (1963) plus MD 520N NOTAR (1989) is the foundational disclosure of (a) the lightweight five-blade-main-rotor light-utility helicopter and (b) circulation-control / Coanda-effect anti-torque without a tail rotor at production scale. Establishes prior art for: (1) NOTAR circulation-controlled anti-torque architecture in a certified production helicopter — a critical prior-art anchor for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'tail-rotor-replaced-by-blown-tail-boom', circulation-control anti-torque, Coanda-effect yaw control, or any 'tailless' yaw architecture using boom-blowing, (2) thrust-vectoring nozzle at the boom tip for fine yaw trim and directional control, (3) noise reduction and ground-handling safety as design drivers for eliminating the tail rotor. McDonnell Douglas NOTAR patents (US 4,200,252 and successors) are long expired. NOTAR is the canonical prior-art reference for circulation-controlled rotorcraft anti-torque and is essential anticipation of multiple modern eVTOL anti-torque claims.

## Bell AH-1 Cobra / SuperCobra / Viper (1965-09-07)

- **id**: `bell-ah-1-cobra`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron / U.S. Army / U.S. Marine Corps
- **disclosure**: Bell Model 209 AH-1G prototype first flight 1965-09-07; entered U.S. Army service 1967 in Vietnam. Twin-engine AH-1J first flight 1969-10-14. Modernized four-blade composite-rotor AH-1Z Viper first flight 2000-12-07, USMC IOC 2010. Total production across all variants exceeds 1,800. Documented in Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron and U.S. Army / USMC technical manuals.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Bell AH-1 Cobra (1965) is the foundational disclosure of the dedicated attack helicopter — the first production rotorcraft designed from scratch around weapons delivery rather than utility transport. Establishes prior art for: (1) narrow tandem-cockpit attack-helicopter configuration (gunner-forward / pilot-aft, both pilots in independent armored cells), (2) chin-mounted slewable weapon turret coupled to gunner sighting, (3) the AH-1Z four-blade composite hingeless main rotor with elastomeric bearings (relevant to modern rigid-rotor eVTOL claims), and (4) stub-wing weapons-station configuration. Bell tandem-cockpit attack patents are long expired. Establishes the attack-helicopter design pattern that AH-64 Apache, Mi-24 Hind, Eurocopter Tiger, and Ka-50 all elaborate.

## MBB Bo 105 (1967-02-16)

- **id**: `mbb-bo-105`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Bölkow / Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm (MBB) (now Airbus Helicopters)
- **disclosure**: MBB Bo 105 V1 prototype first flight 1967-02-16 with a hingeless main rotor; production Bo 105 entered service 1970. ~1,400 produced through 2001. The first production helicopter in the world with a hingeless main rotor — extensively documented in MBB / Eurocopter / Airbus Helicopters technical publications, in the AHS Journal, and in West German Bundeswehr (PAH-1 anti-tank variant) records.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: MBB Bo 105 (1967) is the foundational disclosure of the production hingeless main rotor in a certified rotorcraft. Establishes prior art for: (1) hingeless titanium-hub main rotor — blades bolted rigidly to the hub, flapping/lead-lag accommodated by elastic root deformation; the foundation of all subsequent 'rigid' and 'bearingless' rotor systems, (2) full-aerobatic-envelope rotorcraft including sustained negative-g and inverted flight under power (the Bo 105 was famously aerobatic), (3) elastomeric pitch bearings in place of conventional lubricated bearings (reduced maintenance). MBB hingeless-rotor patents are long expired. The hingeless rotor is the direct ancestor of the bearingless rotors on the EC135/EC145/H135/H145 family and is the canonical prior-art reference for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'rigid main rotor', 'hingeless rotor hub', or 'flexbeam rotor' architectures.

## Mil Mi-24 Hind (1969-09-19)

- **id**: `mil-mi-24-hind`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant (now Russian Helicopters)
- **disclosure**: Mil V-24 prototype first flight 1969-09-19; entered Soviet Air Force service as Mi-24A in 1972; modernized Mi-35M still in production in 2026. Over 2,500 produced across all variants — exported to ~50 countries. Documented in Mil OKB / Russian Helicopters service publications and extensive Western military analysis (Jane's, RAND, IISS).
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Mil Mi-24 Hind (1969) is the foundational disclosure of the combined attack-helicopter + troop-transport hybrid airframe — uniquely combining gunship and IFV roles in one rotorcraft. Establishes prior art for: (1) tandem-stepped armored cockpit attack-helicopter forward fuselage combined with rear troop cabin, (2) high-speed (~180 kt) attack-helicopter with stub wings carrying both lift contribution and weapon stations, (3) the operational concept of combining fire-support with troop-insertion in a single rotorcraft airframe. Mil 1960s patents are long expired. While unusual, the configuration is relevant prior art for any future hybrid attack-utility eVTOL or for claim families combining 'armed VTOL' + 'troop-carrying capacity'.

## Westland Lynx (1971-03-21)

- **id**: `westland-lynx`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Westland Helicopters / Aérospatiale (Anglo-French agreement) / AgustaWestland / Leonardo Helicopters
- **disclosure**: Westland WG.13 Lynx prototype first flight 1971-03-21; entered British Army Air Corps service 1977 and Royal Navy service 1976. BERP-bladed Lynx set the absolute helicopter world speed record at 216.45 kt (400.87 km/h) on 1986-08-11 — a record that stood for over three decades. ~450 produced across all variants. Documented in Westland / AgustaWestland / Leonardo service publications and RAE Farnborough technical reports.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Westland Lynx (1971) is the foundational disclosure of the production four-blade semi-rigid titanium-hub main rotor and (via the 1986 BERP-bladed world-speed-record aircraft) the foundational disclosure of advanced swept-tip rotor-blade aerodynamics in service. Establishes prior art for: (1) four-blade semi-rigid main rotor at 5-ton MTOW class, (2) BERP-style paddle-tip / swept-leading-edge rotor-blade planforms (later adapted into EH101 / AW101 and many subsequent rotors), (3) elastomeric main-rotor and tail-rotor bearings at production scale. Westland rotor patents are long expired. Critical prior-art anchor for any eVTOL claim invoking 'advanced rotor blade planform', 'paddle-tipped rotor', or 'swept-tip lift-rotor blade'.

## Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk / S-70 (1974-10-17)

- **id**: `sikorsky-uh-60-black-hawk`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Sikorsky Aircraft / Lockheed Martin / U.S. Army
- **disclosure**: Sikorsky YUH-60A prototype first flight 1974-10-17 (Utility Tactical Transport Aircraft System (UTTAS) competition); entered U.S. Army service as UH-60A in 1979. UH-60M block-upgrade IOC 2006. Over 4,000 produced across all variants worldwide — the canonical U.S. utility helicopter from 1979 onward. Documented in Sikorsky / Lockheed Martin and U.S. Army TM 1-1520-237 series.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk (1974) is the foundational production disclosure of the modern medium-utility helicopter with canted tail rotor and four-blade fully-articulated titanium-composite main rotor. Establishes prior art for: (1) canted-tail-rotor configuration (anti-torque + lift contribution in a single rotor), (2) four-blade fully-articulated main rotor with elastomeric bearings at medium-utility scale, (3) crashworthy modular construction for utility helicopters (the UH-60 introduced numerous crashworthiness innovations that are now baseline). Sikorsky 1970s patents are long expired. With 4,000+ produced over four decades, the UH-60 family establishes that articulated four-blade rotors and canted tail rotors are public-domain prior art globally.

## Boeing AH-64 Apache (1975-09-30)

- **id**: `boeing-ah-64-apache`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Hughes Helicopters / McDonnell Douglas / Boeing / U.S. Army
- **disclosure**: Hughes YAH-64 prototype first flight 1975-09-30; entered U.S. Army service as AH-64A in 1986. AH-64D Longbow first flight 1992-04-15; AH-64E Guardian 2010-10-26. Over 2,800 produced across all variants. Documented in Boeing / U.S. Army TM 1-1520-251 series and extensive published analysis.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Boeing AH-64 Apache (1975) is the foundational production disclosure of the modern integrated attack-helicopter — combining advanced articulated main rotor, asymmetric-scissors tail rotor, integrated target acquisition / fire-control system, helmet-mounted slew-to-cue weapons, and (in AH-64D) mast-mounted millimeter-wave radar. Establishes prior art for: (1) asymmetric scissors tail-rotor configuration as an acoustic-signature reduction technique (later widely cited in eVTOL low-noise claims), (2) mast-mounted sensor pod above the rotor hub on a production rotorcraft (the Longbow), (3) helmet-mounted-display slew-to-cue weapon-system integration in rotorcraft, (4) integrated targeting/sensor turret slaved to a helmet-tracked head-aim. Hughes/Boeing 1975-1985 patents are long expired. Critical prior-art anchor for eVTOL acoustic-signature claims, mast-mounted sensor claims, and crew-station integration claims.

## Mil Mi-26 Halo (1977-12-14)

- **id**: `mil-mi-26-halo`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant / Rostvertol / Russian Helicopters
- **disclosure**: Mil V-29 / Mi-26 prototype first flight 1977-12-14; entered Soviet Air Force service 1983; production continues at Rostvertol as Mi-26T2V. The largest and heaviest production helicopter ever flown — record-payload demonstrations of 56,768 kg total weight in 1982. Documented in Mil OKB / Russian Helicopters service publications and extensive Western military analysis.

Establishes prior art for: (1) eight-blade fully-articulated main-rotor architecture in service — relevant to any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'high-blade-count main rotor' or 'distributed-loading multi-blade lift rotor', (2) ~20-ton-payload single-rotor heavy-lift configuration, (3) sliding rear ramp-door cargo configuration at airliner scale. Mil 1970s patents are long expired. Critical anticipation of any heavy-lift cargo eVTOL claim invoking large-payload multirotor configurations.

## Sikorsky CH-53K King Stallion (2015-10-27)

- **id**: `sikorsky-ch-53k-king-stallion`
- **corpus**: private
- **creator**: Sikorsky Aircraft / Lockheed Martin / U.S. Marine Corps
- **disclosure**: Sikorsky CH-53K Engineering Development Model 1 first flight 2015-10-27 at Sikorsky's West Palm Beach test facility; entered U.S. Marine Corps service 2022; full operational capability targeted 2029. Sikorsky publicly discloses the seven-blade composite rotor, triplex fly-by-wire controls, and composite airframe construction in U.S. Navy / Marine Corps program documentation and at AHS Forum / Vertical Flight Society technical conferences.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Sikorsky CH-53K King Stallion (2015) is the foundational production disclosure of the seven-blade composite main rotor with triplex fly-by-wire controls in a heavy-lift helicopter. Establishes prior art for: (1) seven-blade composite carbon-fiber main rotor in production at >30-ton MTOW, (2) triplex fly-by-wire flight controls on a heavy-lift production rotorcraft — directly relevant to any eVTOL invoking fly-by-wire triplex flight controls, (3) elastomeric main-rotor-head bearings at heavy-lift scale, (4) composite airframe monocoque at heavy-lift scale. Sikorsky's CH-53K patents are recent and partially active, but the disclosed-and-published architecture is comprehensive prior art for the configuration as a whole. Critical anticipation of any heavy-lift eVTOL claim invoking 'composite rotor blade construction', 'triplex FBW', or 'composite airframe at heavy-lift scale'.
