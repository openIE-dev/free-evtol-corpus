### 1961-07-09 — Mil Mi-8 / Mi-17 (Hip)

- **id:** `mil-mi-8-hip`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant / Kazan Helicopters / Ulan-Ude Aviation Plant
- **disclosure citation:** Mil V-8 prototype first flight 1961-07-09 (single-engine variant); twin-engine V-8A first flight 1962-09-17; entered Soviet Air Force service as Mi-8 in 1967; production continues in 2026 as Mi-171A2/A3. Over 17,000 produced across all variants — more than any other helicopter in history. Operated by ~90 countries. Documented in Mil OKB / Russian Helicopters and Soviet/Russian air-force technical manuals; analyzed in extensive Western literature (Jane's, RAND).
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-articulated`, `anti-torque-tail-rotor`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Mil Mi-8 / Mi-17 (1961) is the foundational disclosure of the highest-production utility-helicopter architecture in history and the canonical Soviet/Russian twin-engine articulated-rotor utility helicopter. Establishes prior art for: (1) five-blade fully-articulated main rotor with elastomeric dampers at production scale, (2) side-by-side twin-turbine arrangement flanking the main gearbox above the cabin (an architecture cloned in many subsequent helicopters), (3) rear clamshell-door cargo configuration for medium-lift utility VTOL. With 17,000+ units produced over six decades, the Mi-8 family establishes that articulated five-blade rotor systems and twin-engine-above-cabin layouts are public-domain prior art globally.

**Sources:**

2. Jane's All The World's Aircraft, multiple editions — Mil Mi-8 / Mi-17 entries.
3. Gordon, Yefim. Mil Mi-8/Mi-17: Rotary-Wing Workhorse and Warhorse. Midland Publishing, 2003.

- **ip status:** patented

The fenestron was first patented by Sud Aviation engineer Paul Fabre in the mid-1960s. Documented in Aérospatiale / Eurocopter technical publications and U.S. Army Foreign Aircraft technical reports.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-articulated`, `anti-torque-fenestron`, `lift-ducted-fan-array`, `cert-part-27`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Aérospatiale SA 341 Gazelle (1967) is the foundational disclosure of the fenestron (ducted-fan anti-torque) system in a production helicopter. Establishes prior art for: (1) ducted-fan-in-fin anti-torque architecture in place of an exposed tail rotor — a critical prior-art anchor for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'ducted-fan tail rotor', 'shrouded anti-torque', or 'integrated fan-in-fin yaw control', (2) noise reduction and ground-strike-hazard reduction as design drivers for shrouded anti-torque, (3) shroud-aerodynamic lift contribution to anti-torque thrust (the duct itself carries ~30-50% of the total side-force). Sud Aviation/Aérospatiale fenestron patents are long expired. The fenestron is the canonical prior-art reference for all ducted anti-torque rotorcraft and anticipates the same architecture used in many modern eVTOL configurations.

**Sources:**
