### 1958-04-22 — Boeing-Vertol CH-46 Sea Knight

- **id:** `boeing-vertol-ch-46-sea-knight`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Vertol Aircraft Corporation / Boeing-Vertol / Boeing / U.S. Navy / U.S. Marine Corps
- **disclosure citation:** Vertol Model 107 prototype first flight 1958-04-22; entered U.S. Marine Corps service as CH-46A Sea Knight in 1964; retired from active U.S. military service 2015 but continues in Japanese / civilian service through 2026. ~620 produced. Documented in Vertol / Boeing-Vertol / Boeing and U.S. Navy / Marine Corps technical publications (NAVAIR 01-250HBB series).
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-tandem-rotor`, `rotor-articulated`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Boeing-Vertol CH-46 Sea Knight (1958) is the foundational disclosure of the medium-twin tandem-rotor helicopter optimized for naval shipboard operations. Establishes prior art for: (1) production tandem-rotor architecture at medium-weight scale (sibling to the heavy-lift CH-47), (2) automatic blade-folding for shipboard parking and hangar stowage of tandem-rotor helicopters, (3) cross-shafted twin-engine architecture in a medium-class tandem-rotor airframe. Vertol 1950s patents are long expired. With CH-47 Chinook (1961), establishes the tandem-rotor configuration as comprehensive prior art across the full helicopter weight spectrum from medium-utility to heavy-lift.

**Sources:**

1. Boeing-Vertol / Boeing CH-46 Sea Knight technical publications.
2. U.S. Navy NAVAIR 01-250HBB Sea Knight maintenance manuals.
3. Spenser, Jay P. Whirlybirds: A History of the U.S. Helicopter Pioneers. University of Washington Press, 1998.

### 1961-09-21 — Boeing CH-47 Chinook

- **id:** `boeing-ch-47-chinook`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Vertol Aircraft Corporation / Boeing Vertol / Boeing / U.S. Army
- **disclosure citation:** Boeing-Vertol YHC-1B (Model 114) prototype first flight 1961-09-21; entered U.S. Army service as CH-47A Chinook in 1962; still in production and active service in 2026 across CH-47F/MH-47G/CH-47F Block II variants. Documented in Boeing-Vertol / Boeing and U.S. Army technical orders, RAND historical analyses, and Smithsonian collections.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-tandem-rotor`, `lift-compound-rotorcraft`, `rotor-articulated`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Boeing CH-47 Chinook (1961) is the foundational production disclosure of the tandem-rotor heavy-lift helicopter at airliner-class payload capacity. Establishes prior art for: (1) production tandem-rotor lift architecture at >20-ton MTOW, (2) two-engine cross-drive synchronized rotor system (single-engine-failure survivability via shared drivetrain), (3) overlapping fore-and-aft rotor disks as torque-canceling lift architecture, (4) ramp-loaded cargo VTOL configuration. Boeing-Vertol tandem-rotor patents are long expired. Direct descendant of Piasecki H-21 (1945). Anticipates: any eVTOL using fore-and-aft lift-rotor pairs or cross-shafted twin-rotor architectures, including multiple modern cargo-eVTOL configurations.

**Sources:**

1. Boeing-Vertol / Boeing CH-47 Chinook technical manuals (U.S. Army TM 1-1520-240 series).
2. RAND Corporation. The Boeing CH-47 Chinook: Origins and Development. 2004.
3. Polmar, Norman and Floyd D. Kennedy. Military Helicopters of the World. Naval Institute Press, 1981.
