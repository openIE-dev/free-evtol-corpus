# Cross-cut: `cert-part-27`

## Bell Model 47 (1945-12-08)

- **id**: `bell-47`
- **corpus**: private
- **creator**: Arthur Young / Bell Aircraft Corporation
- **disclosure**: Bell Model 47 first flight 1945-12-08; received CAA Type Certificate H-1 on 1946-03-08 — the first helicopter in the world to receive a civil type certificate. Over 5,600 built 1946-1973; iconic 'goldfish-bowl' canopy. Used as the H-13 Sioux in U.S. military service 1947-1969; immortalized in the M*A*S*H TV series. Documented in CAA / FAA type certificate records and Bell company archives.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Bell Model 47 (1946) is the first helicopter in the world to receive a civil type certificate (CAA TC H-1, 1946-03-08) — the foundational certified civil rotorcraft prior art and the production validation of Arthur Young's two-blade teetering rotor + stabilizer bar architecture (from bell-model-30 1942). Establishes prior art for: (1) certified civil helicopter operation, (2) Part 27-class rotorcraft type certification basis, (3) production scale deployment of the two-blade teetering rotor architecture. Arthur Young's 1940s-50s patents are long expired. Bell 47 / H-13 Sioux served extensively in Korea and Vietnam as a medevac helicopter, establishing the operational paradigm of small-helicopter rescue operations that the modern eVTOL air-ambulance market is targeting.

## Bell 206 JetRanger / LongRanger (1962-12-08)

- **id**: `bell-206-jetranger`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron / U.S. Army
- **disclosure**: Bell Model 206A JetRanger first flight 1962-12-08 (as YOH-4A Sioux Scout); civil JetRanger redesign first flight 1966-01-10, FAA type-certificated 1966-10-20. OH-58A Kiowa entered U.S. Army service 1969; LongRanger 1974; 206L-4 still in production through Bell's restart. ~7,300 produced across all variants. Documented in Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron type certificate data sheets and U.S. Army TM 55-1520-228 series.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Bell 206 JetRanger (1962) is the foundational disclosure of the production single-turbine light-helicopter configuration that became the workhorse of civil and military light-rotorcraft operations for six decades. Establishes prior art for: (1) production single-turbine + single-tail-rotor light-helicopter architecture under 1,500 kg MTOW, (2) Bell two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor scaled down to light-helicopter category, (3) the canonical Part 27 small-helicopter design baseline. Bell teetering-rotor patents are long expired. With 7,300+ produced over six decades, the 206 family establishes the production-scale civil-certification path that modern Part 27 eVTOL must engage with.

## Hughes/MD 500 (and MD 520N NOTAR) (1963-02-27)

- **id**: `md-500-defender`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Hughes Helicopters / McDonnell Douglas Helicopter Systems / MD Helicopters
- **disclosure**: Hughes 369 (OH-6 Cayuse) first flight 1963-02-27, entered U.S. Army service 1966. Civil/export derivatives Hughes 500 / MD 500 produced under license worldwide. The NOTAR (No Tail Rotor) variant MD 520N first flight 1989-05-01, FAA certified 1991-09-13 — the first and (with the MD 600N) the only NOTAR helicopter to reach civil certification and production. Documented in Hughes / McDonnell Douglas / MD Helicopters technical manuals and U.S. Army TM 55-1520-214 series.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Hughes/MD 500 (1963) plus MD 520N NOTAR (1989) is the foundational disclosure of (a) the lightweight five-blade-main-rotor light-utility helicopter and (b) circulation-control / Coanda-effect anti-torque without a tail rotor at production scale. Establishes prior art for: (1) NOTAR circulation-controlled anti-torque architecture in a certified production helicopter — a critical prior-art anchor for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'tail-rotor-replaced-by-blown-tail-boom', circulation-control anti-torque, Coanda-effect yaw control, or any 'tailless' yaw architecture using boom-blowing, (2) thrust-vectoring nozzle at the boom tip for fine yaw trim and directional control, (3) noise reduction and ground-handling safety as design drivers for eliminating the tail rotor. McDonnell Douglas NOTAR patents (US 4,200,252 and successors) are long expired. NOTAR is the canonical prior-art reference for circulation-controlled rotorcraft anti-torque and is essential anticipation of multiple modern eVTOL anti-torque claims.

## MBB Bo 105 (1967-02-16)

- **id**: `mbb-bo-105`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Bölkow / Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm (MBB) (now Airbus Helicopters)
- **disclosure**: MBB Bo 105 V1 prototype first flight 1967-02-16 with a hingeless main rotor; production Bo 105 entered service 1970. ~1,400 produced through 2001. The first production helicopter in the world with a hingeless main rotor — extensively documented in MBB / Eurocopter / Airbus Helicopters technical publications, in the AHS Journal, and in West German Bundeswehr (PAH-1 anti-tank variant) records.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: MBB Bo 105 (1967) is the foundational disclosure of the production hingeless main rotor in a certified rotorcraft. Establishes prior art for: (1) hingeless titanium-hub main rotor — blades bolted rigidly to the hub, flapping/lead-lag accommodated by elastic root deformation; the foundation of all subsequent 'rigid' and 'bearingless' rotor systems, (2) full-aerobatic-envelope rotorcraft including sustained negative-g and inverted flight under power (the Bo 105 was famously aerobatic), (3) elastomeric pitch bearings in place of conventional lubricated bearings (reduced maintenance). MBB hingeless-rotor patents are long expired. The hingeless rotor is the direct ancestor of the bearingless rotors on the EC135/EC145/H135/H145 family and is the canonical prior-art reference for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'rigid main rotor', 'hingeless rotor hub', or 'flexbeam rotor' architectures.

## Aérospatiale SA 341/342 Gazelle (1967-04-07)

- **id**: `aerospatiale-sa-341-gazelle`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Sud Aviation / Aérospatiale (now Airbus Helicopters) / Westland Helicopters
- **disclosure**: Sud Aviation SA 340 prototype first flight 1967-04-07 (with a conventional tail rotor); SA 341 Gazelle production prototype with fenestron first flight 1968-04-12; entered French Army (ALAT) service 1971, British Army Air Corps service 1973. ~1,775 produced. The fenestron was first patented by Sud Aviation engineer Paul Fabre in the mid-1960s. Documented in Aérospatiale / Eurocopter technical publications and U.S. Army Foreign Aircraft technical reports.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Aérospatiale SA 341 Gazelle (1967) is the foundational disclosure of the fenestron (ducted-fan anti-torque) system in a production helicopter. Establishes prior art for: (1) ducted-fan-in-fin anti-torque architecture in place of an exposed tail rotor — a critical prior-art anchor for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'ducted-fan tail rotor', 'shrouded anti-torque', or 'integrated fan-in-fin yaw control', (2) noise reduction and ground-strike-hazard reduction as design drivers for shrouded anti-torque, (3) shroud-aerodynamic lift contribution to anti-torque thrust (the duct itself carries ~30-50% of the total side-force). Sud Aviation/Aérospatiale fenestron patents are long expired. The fenestron is the canonical prior-art reference for all ducted anti-torque rotorcraft and anticipates the same architecture used in many modern eVTOL configurations.

## Kaman K-MAX (1991-12-23)

- **id**: `kaman-k-max`
- **corpus**: private
- **creator**: Kaman Aircraft Corporation
- **disclosure**: Kaman K-MAX first flight 1991-12-23; FAA Type Certificate Part 27 (restricted category) granted 1994-08-30. Optionally-Piloted Aircraft (OPA) variant flown autonomously by U.S. Marine Corps in Afghanistan 2011-2014 (the first deployed unmanned heavy-lift helicopter).
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Kaman K-MAX is the modern descendant of HH-43 intermeshing-rotor architecture and the first heavy-lift helicopter deployed for unmanned cargo operations in combat (Afghanistan, 2011-2014). Establishes prior art for: (1) modern certified intermeshing-rotor helicopter (extends HH-43 prior art into the 1990s-2010s with documented operational performance), (2) optionally-piloted heavy-lift cargo VTOL (Lockheed Martin retrofit), (3) Part 27 certification basis for intermeshing rotor.

## PAL-V Liberty (2012-04-01)

- **id**: `pal-v-liberty`
- **corpus**: private
- **creator**: PAL-V International B.V. (Raamsdonksveer, Netherlands)
- **disclosure**: PAL-V One prototype first flight 2012-04-01; PAL-V Liberty production design unveiled 2017-03-07 at Geneva Motor Show; received EASA Type Certificate Part 27 (CS-27) for autogyro mode 2024-04-29. PAL-V founded 2008 by John Bakker.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: PAL-V Liberty is the Netherlands' lead drive+fly transformer — architecturally distinct from Slovak peers (Klein/AeroMobil) by using autogyro lift rather than fixed-wing flight. Holds EASA Type Certificate Part 27 (autogyro mode), 2024-04-29 — the world's first type-certified flying car under autogyro classification. Direct architectural descendant of Cierva autogyro (1923) in road-legal passenger form factor.
