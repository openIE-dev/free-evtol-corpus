### 1965-09-07 — Bell AH-1 Cobra / SuperCobra / Viper

- **id:** `bell-ah-1-cobra`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron / U.S. Army / U.S. Marine Corps
- **disclosure citation:** Bell Model 209 AH-1G prototype first flight 1965-09-07; entered U.S. Army service 1967 in Vietnam. Twin-engine AH-1J first flight 1969-10-14. Modernized four-blade composite-rotor AH-1Z Viper first flight 2000-12-07, USMC IOC 2010. Total production across all variants exceeds 1,800. Documented in Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron and U.S. Army / USMC technical manuals.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-semi-rigid-teetering`, `rotor-hingeless`, `anti-torque-tail-rotor`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Bell AH-1 Cobra (1965) is the foundational disclosure of the dedicated attack helicopter — the first production rotorcraft designed from scratch around weapons delivery rather than utility transport. Establishes prior art for: (1) narrow tandem-cockpit attack-helicopter configuration (gunner-forward / pilot-aft, both pilots in independent armored cells), (2) chin-mounted slewable weapon turret coupled to gunner sighting, (3) the AH-1Z four-blade composite hingeless main rotor with elastomeric bearings (relevant to modern rigid-rotor eVTOL claims), and (4) stub-wing weapons-station configuration. Bell tandem-cockpit attack patents are long expired. Establishes the attack-helicopter design pattern that AH-64 Apache, Mi-24 Hind, Eurocopter Tiger, and Ka-50 all elaborate.

1. Bell Helicopter AH-1 Cobra / AH-1Z Viper service publications.
2. U.S. Army TM 1-1520-236 and USMC NAVAIR 01-110HCE technical manuals.

### 1967-02-16 — MBB Bo 105

- **id:** `mbb-bo-105`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Bölkow / Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm (MBB) (now Airbus Helicopters)
- **disclosure citation:** MBB Bo 105 V1 prototype first flight 1967-02-16 with a hingeless main rotor; production Bo 105 entered service 1970. ~1,400 produced through 2001. The first production helicopter in the world with a hingeless main rotor — extensively documented in MBB / Eurocopter / Airbus Helicopters technical publications, in the AHS Journal, and in West German Bundeswehr (PAH-1 anti-tank variant) records.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-hingeless`, `anti-torque-tail-rotor`, `cert-part-27`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> MBB Bo 105 (1967) is the foundational disclosure of the production hingeless main rotor in a certified rotorcraft. Establishes prior art for: (1) hingeless titanium-hub main rotor — blades bolted rigidly to the hub, flapping/lead-lag accommodated by elastic root deformation; the foundation of all subsequent 'rigid' and 'bearingless' rotor systems, (2) full-aerobatic-envelope rotorcraft including sustained negative-g and inverted flight under power (the Bo 105 was famously aerobatic), (3) elastomeric pitch bearings in place of conventional lubricated bearings (reduced maintenance). MBB hingeless-rotor patents are long expired. The hingeless rotor is the direct ancestor of the bearingless rotors on the EC135/EC145/H135/H145 family and is the canonical prior-art reference for any modern eVTOL claim invoking 'rigid main rotor', 'hingeless rotor hub', or 'flexbeam rotor' architectures.

**Sources:**

1. MBB Bo 105 type-certificate data sheet (LBA / FAA).
2. Reichert, G. Hingeless Rotor Technology — A New Way for Helicopters. AHS Journal, 1973.
3. Eurocopter / Airbus Helicopters Bo 105 service publications.

### 1971-03-21 — Westland Lynx

- **id:** `westland-lynx`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Westland Helicopters / Aérospatiale (Anglo-French agreement) / AgustaWestland / Leonardo Helicopters
- **disclosure citation:** Westland WG.13 Lynx prototype first flight 1971-03-21; entered British Army Air Corps service 1977 and Royal Navy service 1976. BERP-bladed Lynx set the absolute helicopter world speed record at 216.45 kt (400.87 km/h) on 1986-08-11 — a record that stood for over three decades. ~450 produced across all variants. Documented in Westland / AgustaWestland / Leonardo service publications and RAE Farnborough technical reports.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-hingeless`, `anti-torque-tail-rotor`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Westland Lynx (1971) is the foundational disclosure of the production four-blade semi-rigid titanium-hub main rotor and (via the 1986 BERP-bladed world-speed-record aircraft) the foundational disclosure of advanced swept-tip rotor-blade aerodynamics in service. Establishes prior art for: (1) four-blade semi-rigid main rotor at 5-ton MTOW class, (2) BERP-style paddle-tip / swept-leading-edge rotor-blade planforms (later adapted into EH101 / AW101 and many subsequent rotors), (3) elastomeric main-rotor and tail-rotor bearings at production scale. Westland rotor patents are long expired. Critical prior-art anchor for any eVTOL claim invoking 'advanced rotor blade planform', 'paddle-tipped rotor', or 'swept-tip lift-rotor blade'.

**Sources:**

1. Westland Helicopters Lynx and Super Lynx service publications.
2. Perry, F.J. and W.J. McDonald. BERP — A Rotor for the Next Generation. Vertica Vol. 10, 1986.
3. Royal Aircraft Establishment Farnborough technical reports on the Lynx high-speed flight tests.
