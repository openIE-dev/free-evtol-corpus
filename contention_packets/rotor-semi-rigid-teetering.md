### 1956-10-22 — Bell UH-1 Iroquois (Huey)

- **id:** `bell-uh-1-iroquois`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Bell Helicopter / U.S. Army
- **disclosure citation:** Bell XH-40 (Model 204) prototype first flight 1956-10-22; entered U.S. Army service as HU-1 (later UH-1) Iroquois in 1959; ~16,000 produced through 1976 with continuing modernized variants in service into the 2020s. The single most-produced military helicopter in history and the defining turbine-powered utility helicopter of the Vietnam War. Documented in Bell Helicopter and U.S. Army technical orders, Smithsonian collections, and exhaustive published literature.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-semi-rigid-teetering`, `anti-torque-tail-rotor`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Bell UH-1 Iroquois (1956) is the foundational disclosure of the production turbine-powered utility helicopter and the two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor at production scale. Establishes prior art for: (1) two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor architecture (simpler than articulated, lighter than rigid; the basis for almost all subsequent Bell helicopters including 206, 212, 412, 222, 230, 407, 412EPI), (2) single-turbine + tail-rotor utility-helicopter configuration at production scale, (3) the cable-with-hydraulic-boost flight-control architecture that dominated helicopter design before fly-by-wire. Bell teetering-rotor patents are long expired. With 16,000+ units produced over six decades of service, the UH-1 establishes that two-blade teetering rotors are public-domain prior art for any eVTOL claim invoking a teetering main rotor or simple semi-rigid rotor hub.

**Sources:**

1. Bell Helicopter UH-1 Iroquois technical manuals (U.S. Army TM 55-1520-210 series).
2. U.S. Army Center of Military History, Air Cavalry in Vietnam.
3. Spenser, Jay P. Whirlybirds: A History of the U.S. Helicopter Pioneers. University of Washington Press, 1998.

**Prior art notes:**

Establishes prior art for: (1) production single-turbine + single-tail-rotor light-helicopter architecture under 1,500 kg MTOW, (2) Bell two-blade semi-rigid teetering main rotor scaled down to light-helicopter category, (3) the canonical Part 27 small-helicopter design baseline. Bell teetering-rotor patents are long expired. With 7,300+ produced over six decades, the 206 family establishes the production-scale civil-certification path that modern Part 27 eVTOL must engage with.

### 1965-09-07 — Bell AH-1 Cobra / SuperCobra / Viper

- **id:** `bell-ah-1-cobra`
- **corpus:** academic
- **ip status:** patented
- **creator:** Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron / U.S. Army / U.S. Marine Corps
- **disclosure citation:** Bell Model 209 AH-1G prototype first flight 1965-09-07; entered U.S. Army service 1967 in Vietnam. Twin-engine AH-1J first flight 1969-10-14. Modernized four-blade composite-rotor AH-1Z Viper first flight 2000-12-07, USMC IOC 2010. Total production across all variants exceeds 1,800. Documented in Bell Helicopter / Bell Textron and U.S. Army / USMC technical manuals.
- **disclosed subsystems:** `lift-coaxial-rotor`, `rotor-semi-rigid-teetering`, `rotor-hingeless`, `anti-torque-tail-rotor`, `cert-military`

**Prior art notes:**

> Bell AH-1 Cobra (1965) is the foundational disclosure of the dedicated attack helicopter — the first production rotorcraft designed from scratch around weapons delivery rather than utility transport. Establishes prior art for: (1) narrow tandem-cockpit attack-helicopter configuration (gunner-forward / pilot-aft, both pilots in independent armored cells), (2) chin-mounted slewable weapon turret coupled to gunner sighting, (3) the AH-1Z four-blade composite hingeless main rotor with elastomeric bearings (relevant to modern rigid-rotor eVTOL claims), and (4) stub-wing weapons-station configuration. Bell tandem-cockpit attack patents are long expired. Establishes the attack-helicopter design pattern that AH-64 Apache, Mi-24 Hind, Eurocopter Tiger, and Ka-50 all elaborate.

**Sources:**

1. Bell Helicopter AH-1 Cobra / AH-1Z Viper service publications.
2. U.S. Army TM 1-1520-236 and USMC NAVAIR 01-110HCE technical manuals.
3. Bishop, Chris. Huey Cobra Gunships. Osprey, 2006.
